How Fair and Square was made

It looks simple. It wasn't the first try. Seven versions, June to October 2026, and what each one left behind. About me

  1. July

    An essay called “Merit or Math?”

    • Kept: the question.
    • Let go: most of the prose. The pictures would have to do the explaining.
  2. July

    A room of shapes, trading

    • Kept: the room, and area as money.
    • Let go: the triangles, hexagons and circles. A square's side is the square root of its area, so one picture can show money as an area or as a side; and equal squares fill the box without gaps.
  3. August

    A cast of scientists, and a spherical cow

    • Kept: the joke's lesson. Simplify until the maths fits, then say so.
    • Let go: the cast.
  4. September

    Blue and Red, arguing

    • Kept: the colours.
    • Let go: the narrator. I wanted the yard to do the telling.
  5. Early October

    A studio for faces

    • Kept: faces that feel the money, on two axes from Russell's map of feelings.
    • Let go: seven of the eight styles.
  6. 10 October

    One room, two dials

    • Kept: the dials, now the stake and the tax, and the charts beside the room.
    • Let go: the speech bubbles.
  7. 18 October

    Fair and Square

    No narrator. A coin, some squares, and pictures that are the statistics themselves. Play it.

Not every step was progress, and some of what went may come back in another piece.

Inventory: 381 commits across all branches, dated 21 June to 22 October 2026 (commit dates, not hours). The August drawings are from the process archive. Written with help from AI coding assistants.
